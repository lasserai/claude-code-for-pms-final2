Rook Dispatch · final report

Release 4.2 didn't have a seasonal dip.
It switched four people off.

What happened, how six sessions found it, what the fix looks like in the prototype, and the checks that keep it from happening again.

Dispatch PMSix modules · 10–24 Sep 2026Evidence: tickets, interviews, callout-history.csv, routing code

The plain answer

A shorter timeout set off a scoring flaw that had been there since 2019

14.2 cuts the timeoutOffers now expire after 60s instead of 90s. More offers go unanswered.
2Each miss costs more than a yes earns−0.12 for a miss, +0.08 for an accept, with no decay back to neutral.
3Standing sinksMisses pile up. With 4.2 also weighting distance more, nearby responders outrank them.
4Fewer offersLow standing means they're asked last, or not at all.
5No way backThe only thing that raises standing is answering an offer they no longer get.

Four healthy responders (Farlight, Meteor Mite, The Undertow, Vesper) fell to 0–1 offers a week. Their work went to others, and the headline acceptance rate is climbing back, so the average hides them.

Separate issueNine other responders say "nothing's coming through" while records show offers sent. That points to push delivery (4.2 also changed duplicate-push handling), not ranking. It has its own audit.

Weekly acceptance rate, all responders

77% → 54% in the week of 4.2 → back to 73%. It looks almost fixed.

What it costs

Work didn't disappear. It moved.

Total offers held at 158–177 a week throughout, so this isn't a slow August. The same work was spread across fewer people.

25%of responders (4 of 16) fell to 0–1 offers a week. We bill per active responder.
32→48%share of all offers going to the top 4 responders, within three weeks
~100fewer accepted offers in 4 weeks (−19%). Whether that equals unfilled callouts is still for Ravi to confirm.
~3×normal callout ticket volume. One affected responder never filed a ticket at all.

Origin Story · the inherited story

We started with "mostly seasonal"

The handover explained it simply: August is always soft, the shorter timeout adds to it, wait for September's numbers, and don't let this become a conversation about reverting 4.2.

The first fix direction followed from that story: ease the timeout from 60s to 75s, treat "offer never arrived" as a delivery question, and leave the ranking weights alone.

Two of those survived: the 75s ease and the delivery audit. The "seasonal" part didn't.

Artifact: "4.2 fix direction (tentative)" in CLAUDE.md, and Priya's handover.

Super Hearing · the feedback

Two piles that seemed to disagree were both right

The tickets split about 2:1 between "offer never arrived" and "it expired before I could answer." Only the second was explained by the timeout.

The interviews added what the tickets missed. Aunt Dot mentioned "quiet weeks" in a console-design interview. Her responder, Vesper, was one of the four who collapsed, and never filed a single ticket.

Both piles were true at once: some people were getting offers they couldn't catch, and others had stopped getting offers at all.

Sources: tickets T-001–T-025, interviews by Sofia Marino (2–5 Sep).

My prompt05-super-speed · #7
If the responder has not gotten any offers past 7 days their scoring position will be neutral so that they are penalized for good. Create specification for that feature and implement it to the prototype.
  • Handlers are told. A flag appears when a responder falls below 40% of their own usual offers. Replayed on the real data, it catches exactly the four, in the week of 17 Aug, and no one else.
  • A plain reason, or an honest "we can't confirm delivery" for the other group.
  • "I'm back — send me work" restores standing right away. It's worth about 9 minutes of distance, not a guarantee of work.
  • A 7-day automatic reset, so nobody is penalised forever.

Artifacts: prototype.html (all 16 responders, a +1 day clock, region view), auto-reset-spec.md, brief.md.
